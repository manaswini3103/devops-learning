# CAMs Priciples

CAMS is a foundational model that explains the core principles of DevOps. It was introduced by John Willis and Damon Edwards to describe what DevOps focuses on beyond just tools.

CAMS = Culture, Automation, Measurement, Sharing


## Culture

- Breaking down silos between Development and Operations teams and encouraging, communicative, and transparent culture.
- The goal is to foster a shared sense of responsibility and mutual understanding, where all team members work toward the common goal of delivering value to the customer.
- Developers and Ops jointly own production issues instead of blaming each other.

## Automation

- Automation is a key technical practice in DevOps, aiming to minimize manual, repetitive tasks and reduce the risk of human error.
- This principle applies to all stages of the software delivery lifecycle, including automated testing, continuous integration (CI) and continuous delivery (CD) pipelines, infrastructure as code, and automated deployments.
- Automation increases efficiency, speed, reliability, and repeatability of processes.

## Measurement

- To determine if progress is being made in the right direction, teams must measure everything.
- This goes beyond just infrastructure metrics (like CPU load or server utilization) to include business metrics (customer satisfaction, revenue), performance metrics (deployment frequency, lead time, mean time to recovery/MTTR), and team metrics (employee satisfaction).

## Sharing

- Sharing involves promoting transparency and openness across all teams and stakeholders.
- This includes sharing knowledge, ideas, problems, feedback, tools, and responsibilities.
- By sharing insights and lessons learned (even from mistakes), teams can tighten feedback loops, avoid redundant work, and continuously improve the collective intelligence and efficiency of the organization
